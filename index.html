Mathematical Background & Theory

A comprehensive calculus guide connecting container geometry $r(y)$, cross-sectional area $A(h)$, and depth rate of change $\frac{dh}{dt}$.

Calculus Analysis for Selected Container

Active Container Derivation

1. Volume by Disk Method

A container generated by rotating a radial boundary curve $r(y)$ around the vertical $y$-axis forms a Solid of Revolution.

$$V(h) = \int_0^h A(y) \, dy = \pi \int_0^h [r(y)]^2 \, dy$$

Where $A(y) = \pi [r(y)]^2$ represents the horizontal circular cross-sectional area at height $y$.

2. Rate of Depth Change ($\frac{dh}{dt}$)

Liquid enters at a constant volumetric rate $Q = \frac{dV}{dt}$. Applying the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and the Chain Rule:

$$\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dh} \cdot \frac{dh}{dt} \implies Q = A(h) \cdot \frac{dh}{dt}$$
$$\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{Q}{A(h)} = \frac{Q}{\pi [r(h)]^2}$$
